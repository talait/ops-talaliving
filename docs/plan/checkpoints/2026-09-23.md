# D14 — the walkthrough, and what Phase 1 actually bought

*2026-09-23 · walked on the deployed branch, every figure below read off the
running app rather than typed from memory.*

---

## The walk, in the order the business works

Nine screens, which is also `?tour=flow-b` in the app itself.

**1 · A request is a line, not a document** — `/procurement/pr`
Five lines are waiting for a decision, Rp 7.912.000 asked for. Open one and
the status, the approval trail, what was actually paid against it and every
attached file are all on the line, because the line is the thing anybody ever
asks a question about.

**2 · The meeting decides** — `/procurement/meeting`
Tick what should be approved; the total to pay and the BCA 271 balance sit
*above* the list, so the room sees whether the money exists before anybody
says yes.

**3 · The approver answers, not the laptop** — `/demo/chat`
One card, its totals, a random token. The name written onto the approval is
whoever answered it. This was the single most valuable correction of the whole
fortnight (F16): nothing was broken, and every approval was attributed to the
wrong person.

**4 · Paid from the line** — back to `/procurement/pr`
The ledger row is written from the line, with its evidence, in one act.
Nothing is marked paid because somebody remembers paying it.

**5 · Money out, with proof and detail** — `/accounting/ledger`
34 rows across five accounts. A row cannot be posted without at least one
nota, transfer proof or photo; a purchase must say what, how many, at what
price, from whom. Cash position across PETTY CASH, BNI 325, BCA 271, BCA 064
and JAGO at the top.

**6 · Goods against money** — `/procurement/tracker`
Contracted Rp 233.445.000 · paid Rp 76.553.000 · **owed Rp 156.892.000** · of
which **Rp 30.229.500 is billable today**. Two numbers nobody had before, and
they are not the same size. Recording a delivery needs the photo *and* the
tanda terima, the receiver and the QC.

**7 · The exception road** — `/accounting/verifikasi`
Five roads out of a context-free photo, none of which delete. Rejecting never
reaches the ledger — asked directly, and the answer surprised the asker (F26).

**8 · Where the last transfer went** — `/accounting/liquidation`
Seven transfers in, Rp 220.200.000. Three were spent through before the next
one arrived; the 20 August transfer of Rp 29.100.000 lasted **one day**, and
Rp 2.661.000 went out after it from the balance already there. Rp 21.720.000
of purchases had no approved line or order behind them.

**9 · Does the money last** — `/accounting/calendar`
Cash today Rp 134.173.000. Twelve months of bills against twelve months of
transfers: **the money runs out in November 2026, Rp 17.961.000 short**, and
every month after is worse. Open December and it says more than that — the
month *breaks on the 18th*, on a payroll run, five days before the figure the
month view shows.

## What Phase 1 bought

Thirty-two findings, and the honest summary is that **six of them were not
about the software at all.** They were things about the business that nobody
had written down, which surfaced only because a screen had to display
something:

- an approval was being attributed to whoever opened the laptop (F16)
- over-delivery was being counted as value received, which quietly turns an
  unasked-for delivery into money a vendor can invoice (F27 / D98)
- a deposit was being billed on orders nobody had sent (F27 / D99)
- the demo's own fixture claimed a client payment into an account that has
  never received one — the business is funded by its owner, not by clients,
  and a schema built on the other assumption would have been wrong at the root
  (F29)
- payroll is weekly, and writing it as a monthly figure hid four whole payroll
  runs a year (F31)
- Rp 156,9 juta of supplier obligations have no date on them at all, so no
  cash plan can hold them (F30)

None of those cost a migration. All of them would have.

## What the walk could not answer

Three things the demo makes visible and cannot fix, all of which belong to
Phase 2 or to the owner:

1. **No date on a payment term.** The calendar states the undated pool rather
   than spreading it. Schema change §1.
2. **No pairing between the two halves of an internal transfer.** The
   liquidation report matches on amount and date, and says so. Schema §2.
3. **Who may read the access log, and for how long is it kept** (Q22). This is
   policy, not a technical choice, and defaulting it quietly is how a
   surveillance decision gets made by accident.

## What we would do differently

Build the *hardest-to-fake* screen first. The purchase tracker and the payment
calendar produced four of the six business findings above, because both had to
show a number that could be checked against reality. The screens that mostly
list and filter produced almost none.

The corollary, which is F32: **the pages nobody argues about are the pages
nobody checks.** The dashboard was still showing invented sales orders on
D13 — not because anyone defended it, but because it had never been asked a
question it could get wrong.
